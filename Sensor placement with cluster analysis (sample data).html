Sensor placement with cluster analysisSample data

A sample 14-dome, 1-acre polyhouse has 42 temperature and humidity sensors: one on the south side, one in the centre and one on the north side of every dome. Clustering groups the sensors that behave alike, so one sensor per group can stand in for the rest. Change the number of sensors to see which locations are critical, how much accuracy is lost and what the sensors cost.

Clusters and the sensor to keep

How it works: for each sensor, four numbers describe its 14 days of hourly readings: average and daily swing of temperature, and the same for humidity. Sensors are then grouped by Ward hierarchical clustering. In each group, the sensor closest to the group’s centre is the one to keep. Each other sensor is estimated from the kept one, adjusted for its usual difference. The readings are randomly generated, with a warm zone in the centre-west domes, a cool zone near the east end, and different behaviour on the south side, centre and north side. Nothing here comes from a real polyhouse.
