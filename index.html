About me

YS

I’m a Senior Data Analyst based in Kota, Rajasthan, working with agricultural data at Eeki Automation. My days are spent with IoT sensor, climate and crop data, turning it into dashboards and tools that farm teams rely on.

My path began in electrical engineering, working with PLC and SCADA systems, and continued through teaching as an assistant professor. Those years taught me to explain complex ideas simply, and to respect how machines and people work together.

Today I lead a team of 7 data professionals, write standard procedures for collecting reliable data, and keep building on the side, from a weather forecasting dashboard to a crop health monitoring system.

To me, data work is only finished when someone uses it to make a better decision.

Sensor placement with cluster analysis

Built with sample data

A sample 14-dome, 1-acre polyhouse with 42 temperature and humidity sensors. Clustering groups the sensors that behave alike and picks the critical locations to keep, with a plan view of the polyhouse, an accuracy curve against random picks, and the sensor cost for any number of sensors.

At work
Cluster analysis of temperature and humidity data from 42 sensors (south, centre and north of every dome) to find the critical locations, cutting the sensors from 42 to 5 per acre while staying representative of the whole acre. Repeated for fan-pad and naturally ventilated polyhouses, because the best locations depend on the sensor data, the structure and the operations
In this demo
A polyhouse map with colour-coded clusters, the critical sensors to keep, accuracy against random picks, and the cost saved for any number of sensors
Tools
HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with Ward hierarchical clustering written in JavaScript. Sample data made with a seeded random generator in JavaScript

Models and analysis

Crop yield forecasting

Built with sample data

Weekly harvest forecasts 1 to 8 weeks ahead for 20 sample polyhouses growing tomato and cucumber. Four models are trained in Python on 3 years of weekly records and tested on a held-out year. The best model cuts the error of a naive forecast by 25% (1 week ahead) to 74% (8 weeks ahead), and every forecast comes with an 80% range.

At work
Yield forecasts from climate and crop data, used by production, operations and sales teams to plan harvests, resources and orders. This improved operational efficiency and reduced losses
In this demo
An 8-week harvest plan, a forecast with an 80% range for each polyhouse, a year-long backtest, a comparison of four models at every distance ahead, accuracy by crop, and the inputs that drive the forecast
Tools
Python (pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn): ridge regression, random forest and gradient boosting, time-based backtesting, time-ordered cross-validation and grouped permutation importance. Dashboard in HTML, CSS, JavaScript and SVG. Sample data made with a seeded generator in Python
